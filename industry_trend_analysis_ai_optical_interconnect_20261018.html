1. Introduction

  • What it is: the light-based links that connect GPUs, switches and racks inside AI data centers: indium phosphide (InP) lasers (EML and CW types), 800G and 1.6T pluggable transceivers, and co-packaged optics (CPO) that put the optics next to the switch chip.
  • Why now: TrendForce expects the AI optical-transceiver market to grow from $16.5B in 2025 to $26B in 2026, and Coherent says 1.6T ramped sooner than it expected three months earlier. Nvidia has started shipping its Spectrum-X CPO switch and Broadcom's 51.2T CPO switch is in volume manufacturing.
  • The constraint: laser output, not module assembly. Lumentum's CEO says it ships more than 30% below customer demand, and Coherent says laser output is its datacenter revenue ceiling. TrendForce counts Broadcom, Lumentum and Sumitomo Electric as roughly 55% of EML and CW laser supply.
  • End state: most AI cluster links above a few meters run on optics, CPO takes the scale-up links, and lasers become the scarce, contract-allocated input the way advanced-node wafers are for GPUs. Copper survives on the shortest links.
  • Investment opportunity: results are already showing it: Lumentum's fiscal Q4 revenue was about $1.01B (+109% YoY) with a $1.225B–$1.275B guide, and Coherent's was $2.05B (+34%) with a $2.2B–$2.4B guide (company releases, Aug 2026). Unlike 2000, the leaders are profitable and carry take-or-pay contracts, but the buyer base now includes debt-funded neoclouds.
  • Common misconception: the AI selloff of Oct 12–16 (Nasdaq −4.5% on the week; LITE −25% and COHR −25% in five sessions) is not evidence the shortage ended. It is a financing scare; shortage evidence (booked-out capacity) did not change.

4. TAM Expansion Analysis

4a. Market Size & Growth Drivers

Optical links were a niche networking cost; AI clusters turn them into a per-GPU cost line, because a training rack needs many more optical connections than a web server. Forecasts differ widely because they define the market differently, so the bullets below keep each source's scope.

  • AI optical transceivers: $16.5B (2025) to $26B (2026), about +58% (TrendForce, 2026).
  • 800G-and-above transceivers: $45.2B (2026) to $129.5B (2028); total optical transceivers about $148.5B by 2028 (Goldman Sachs via Futu News). Note this is far above TrendForce's scope; the two are not reconciled here.
  • Data-center transceivers: RMB 114.9B (2025) to RMB 429.4B (2030) (Frost & Sullivan via an HKEX filing; roughly $16B to $60B at about 7.2 RMB per dollar, our conversion).
  • Low case: $9.7B (2025) to $24.7B (2032), a 14.3% CAGR (P&S Intelligence).
  • Laser supply: EML plus CW-DFB capacity of about 50.7 million units per month in 2026, roughly double the prior level (TrendForce).
  • New formats: Coherent puts CPO, near-packaged optics and related formats at more than $15B of incremental addressable market (company, Aug 2026).

Structural drivers are the number of GPUs per cluster, rack power density (about 27 kW on average in 2026 vs 7 kW in 2021 per one source), and the move to 1.6T and CPO because copper cannot carry that bandwidth beyond short distances; these are physical, not sentiment. Cyclical drivers are hyperscaler capex budgets and neocloud financing, which this week showed can tighten quickly. The wide forecast range is itself a warning: the true TAM is highly sensitive to whether AI cluster build-out continues at the 2026 pace.

6d. False-Alarm Test

  • Are the fundamentals confirming? Yes so far: fiscal Q4 results and guides were raised in August (Lumentum $1.25B midpoint, Coherent $2.2B–$2.4B). Check Nov 4–5 for booked-out language, backlog repricing and gross margin; this week's drop came with no change in order data.
  • Theme-specific or market-wide? Both: the Nasdaq fell 4.5% on the week and LITE and COHR fell about 25%, while the Dow rose 0.7%, so it is an AI-complex move with a rate backdrop. Compare the basket to the Nasdaq and to HY OAS.
  • Structural or cyclical signals? Supply catch-up (Signal 2) and margin erosion (Signal 4) would be structural; crowding (3) and macro (6) are cyclical and usually reverse.
  • Has the end state changed? No: the end state (optics on most AI cluster links, CPO for scale-up) is on the same timeline; Coherent pulled 1.6T forward. If CPO uses far fewer lasers per port than expected, the end state for lasers changes.

6e. Closest Historical Analogue

The closest peak is the optical-components boom of 2000, with JDS Uniphase as the bellwether. It peaked in March 2000 (about $153 per share per one source) and a buyer at the peak was still down nearly 98% years later (Globe and Mail). Its revenue fell from $3.2B in the year to June 2001 to $676M on a trailing basis by 2003 after telecom customers such as Lucent and Nortel hit an earnings wall. The comparison is unflattering where it counts: 2000 demand was financed by debt and vendor financing, and today's neocloud debt and Nvidia's reported equity stakes in suppliers echo that. The difference is that today's leaders earn 22%–37% non-GAAP operating margins and have take-or-pay contracts, and their largest buyers are cash-generating hyperscalers.
