Scuttlebutt verdict: STRONG (with two soft spots)

  • AI demand is contracted, not hoped for. $179.2B of remaining obligations and named multi-gigawatt programs at four customers.
  • Google, the anchor customer, is deliberately multi-sourcing. Management frames MediaTek's TPU win as a pricing outcome; that still caps the Google share Broadcom keeps.
  • VMware customers stay because leaving is hard, not because they are happy. That is real pricing power but a long-run reputational cost.
  • Culture is a known trade-off: lean, high-pressure and acquisitive. It works for margins, but it is not Fisher's 'outstanding' personnel relations.

Key Risks and What to Monitor

  • AI capex cycle and concentration: six customers, the largest an AI lab (Anthropic) whose own funding depends on capital markets.
  • Multi-sourcing: Google's TPU is split across Broadcom, MediaTek and Marvell (and reportedly AMD). Customers own the architecture, so Broadcom is a design partner, not a platform owner.
  • Customer financing: the AI XPV backstop on 5-year leases; maximum exposure UNVERIFIED.
  • Margin mix: XPUs and rack-level content carry lower gross margin than software and networking (~500 bp YoY decline).
  • VMware reputation and regulators: EU complaints and migrations could cap software renewals after the current contracts.
