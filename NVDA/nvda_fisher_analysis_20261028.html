Scuttlebutt verdict: STRONG

  • Every channel confirms the franchise. Customers order the next generation before it ships, employees rate it a top-3 employer, and it keeps beating its own guidance.
  • The cracks are in how demand is financed, not in demand itself. Longer payment terms, $108.5B of guarantees and customer equity stakes mean NVIDIA is starting to fund its own order book.
  • Competition is real at the edges. Meta's AMD commitment and ASICs growing ~3x faster than GPUs are where share will move first, mainly in inference.
  • Memory is the near-term margin swing factor. Management has said so up front.

Key Risks and What to Monitor

  • AI capex cycle: NVIDIA's earnings are its customers' capex. A digestion year after FY2028 is the bear case, and FY2023 shows the drop can be −55%.
  • Customers become competitors: the four biggest buyers all run custom-silicon programs, and Meta's 6 GW AMD commitment shows share is up for grabs.
  • Customer financing: up to $108.5B of guarantees plus $65B of cloud agreements against $56.6B of cash and debt securities, with one counterparty (OpenAI) behind the largest.
  • Supply chain: HBM4 timing (SK Hynix base-die redesign risk), CoWoS capacity and memory pricing all hit Rubin volume and margin.
  • Geopolitics: China is out of guidance, and SAMR antitrust plus Taiwan concentration at TSMC are tail risks.
