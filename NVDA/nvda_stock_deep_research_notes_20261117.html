Investment Thesis

NVIDIA is the toll road of the artificial intelligence (AI) build-out, and the market has just marked it at a distressed-cyclical multiple for a governance scandal that has not touched the earnings. Q3 FY2027 revenue of $115.84B (+103% Year-over-Year (YoY), +20% Quarter-over-Quarter (QoQ)) came with a record 66.9% operating margin and a Data Center line that accelerated to +21% QoQ as Vera Rubin did ~$20B in its first quarter. Management then guided Q4 to $130B and raised FY2028 growth to ~75%. Yet at $155.46 the stock trades at 9.8x FY2028E Earnings Per Share (EPS), and a reverse Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) shows the price only works if FY2028 revenue falls ~10% below FY2027. The anchoring metric is that 9.8x forward multiple against a ~65% FY2028 EPS growth rate: a Price/Earnings-to-Growth (PEG) ratio of ~0.15–0.25. We are long, but sized as a starter: the ~$640B stack of purchase commitments, guarantees and customer equity stakes means NVIDIA is now partly financing its own demand, and that is the risk the CEO vacuum makes harder to monitor.

Business & Competitive Position

  • What the company does: sells the full AI-factory platform: Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), Vera CPUs, NVLink and Spectrum-X networking, and the CUDA software stack. Data Center was $107.6B, 92.9% of Q3 revenue, and NVIDIA holds an estimated ~80–85% of AI accelerator spend (Silicon Analysts / Morgan Stanley, 2026E).
  • Moat: three layers that compound. CUDA developer lock-in; rack-scale systems (Vera Rubin NVL72) that competitors sell as parts; and supply allocation, where $390B of purchase commitments lock up high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and advanced packaging for 2027. The proof is pricing: Hopper was recontracted at up to 2x its original price and B300 rental rates rose 60%+ a generation after launch.
  • Key risk to the moat: customer concentration funding custom silicon. Hyperscale is ~$60B (~56% of Data Center), one direct customer was 16% of Q2 revenue, and those same buyers are scaling Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) and Trainium. NVIDIA's answer is to finance AI labs and AI cloud providers (guarantees of $118B), which deepens dependence on the same customers.
