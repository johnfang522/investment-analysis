  • Concentration: the Anthropic commitment alone is about $1.66B a year by simple division against $4.32B of TTM revenue. Customer concentration for the other CIS deals is not disclosed (a US technology customer committed more than $600M over four years for robotics development).
  • Take-or-pay: reduces demand risk but not counterparty risk; Anthropic is a private company and a warrant structure ties about 2% of Akamai's shares to the initial commitment, plus another 3% to up to $9B of additional purchases (GuruFocus).

AI Moat Assessment

  • Positional, not structural: the AI moat is its footprint and security attach, not data, models or silicon. Security (Guardicore, WAF, API security) is the durable asset: management cited a CrowdStrike win and DDoS attack scale up about 10x in a year.
  • Hyperscaler threat: high for compute, moderate for security. The chokepoint analysis does not support a moat claim for CIS.
  • AI-washing check: not AI-washing, because there are signed contracts. It is capital-intensity risk dressed as an AI story.

Go-To-Market

  • Enterprise-led security and platform sales; CIS deals are large, lumpy, multi-year commitments. Sales efficiency metrics (magic number, CAC payback) are not disclosed.

  • Execution: the Q4 'hockey stick'; delayed GPU shipments already pushed CIS acceleration out a quarter.
  • Customer concentration: Anthropic would be about 38% of TTM revenue on a simple division; it is private and the contract includes a warrant.

  • Legacy decline: delivery revenue −6%, and the security line must keep growing 10% to offset.
  • Regulatory: not a primary risk here; export controls on GPUs matter for supply, not demand.
