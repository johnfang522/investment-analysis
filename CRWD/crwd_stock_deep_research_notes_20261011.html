Investment Thesis

CrowdStrike is the best-run franchise in security priced as if the best case were already certain. Our reverse Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) needs about 46% annual FCF growth for five years against a 22.5% consensus revenue path, and our probability-weighted FY+5 price is $136. We PASS: too expensive to own, too strong and momentum-driven (+115.7% over 12 months against SPY +16.9%) to short without a catalyst.

Business & Competitive Position

  • What the company does: CrowdStrike sells a cloud-native endpoint, cloud, identity and Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platform (Falcon) on subscription, with $5.84B of ending ARR and $5.40B of TTM revenue (SEC EDGAR; CrowdStrike Q2 FY27 release).
  • Moat: a single-agent, single-platform architecture with module-attach economics and high switching costs; a 75.2% gross margin and RPO of $10.7B growing faster than revenue show customers committing for longer (Appendices A, D).
  • Key risk to the moat: Microsoft bundling pressure on endpoint security, compounded by the possibility that Q2's Falcon Flex strength was commitment pull-forward rather than share gain; weak Flex re-commit rates are the single tripwire (Appendix A).
