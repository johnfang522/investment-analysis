Current conditions most resemble January 2022: valuation and leverage at records, a Fed beginning a tightening cycle, credit starting to widen and the leadership group (then long-duration tech, now AI semis) breaking first. That episode became a −25% bear market over ten months. The analogy breaks down in two places. Credit is far from stressed (352 bps against ~590 at the 2022 high), and AI earnings are still growing quickly (Nvidia's Q4 guide of ~$130B). The more likely path is a valuation-led bear market amplified by deleveraging, not a credit crisis. The risk is skewed toward a larger drawdown than this 11.6% so far: CAPE at 36 is still far above where 2022 ended (~28).

Overall Market Opinion

A composite of 38 puts sentiment in the Bearish zone, down 9 points in a week. The S&P 500 fell 8.6%, its worst week since April 2025, and closed 11.6% below its Oct 6 high after seven straight losses. Fear readings are at extremes: Fear & Greed 9.7 and VIX 35.89, both the worst of the cycle. On their own those would argue for buying. What changes the call is that this time the bond market agrees with the equity market.

The credit signal is the most important development since the last run. HY spreads widened on four straight days to 352 bps, through the 350 bps line the Nov 5 crash stopped short of, and CCC spreads are at 1,361 bps. That was before Friday's −3.7% session. On the Treasury side, the 10Y fell 24 bp to 4.91% as investors fled to safety. The 2Y at 4.76% and a ~95%-priced December hike show this is fear, not a Fed pivot. Equal-weight fell 6.2% and gold fell more than 9%, consistent with broad deleveraging. Margin debt, at a record share of GDP, started contracting in October, before this selloff.

Over the next 3–6 months the Fed is the problem. The October minutes point to more hikes, Waller wants 50 bp or a January commitment, and a $2.1T deficit with possible coupon upsizing leaves no room for a fiscal rescue. Hikes into a correction with record leverage are how 2022 developed. The Nov 25 PCE print and the Dec 9 dots decide whether this stays a correction or becomes a bear market. Overall verdict: cautious — Risk-Off, conviction 6. Cut net exposure to low net-long, reduce gross, keep index hedges and underweight AI semis and long-duration growth. Do not chase shorts after a single-digit Fear & Greed reading; use rallies to reduce risk. Return to Neutral when HY OAS is back under ~330 bps and VIX under 25. Cut further if HY OAS goes above 400 bps or the Fed hikes 50 bp.
