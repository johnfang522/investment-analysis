AI Custom Silicon (XPU/ASIC) & Scale-Out Networking

Triggered by: AVGO — mapped to AI Custom Silicon (XPU/ASIC) & Scale-Out Networking · Buy-side thematic read · October 9, 2026 · Theme Conviction 6/10 · Peak verdict: Peaking (3/6 exhaustion signals firing)

1. Introduction

  • What it is: hyperscalers and frontier labs designing their own AI accelerators (XPUs/ASICs: Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Meta MTIA, Microsoft Maia, OpenAI's custom chip) with a design partner (mainly Broadcom and Marvell), tied together by Ethernet-based scale-out and scale-up networking instead of InfiniBand and merchant-GPU stacks.
  • Why now: Broadcom's FQ3 FY2026 (quarter ended Aug 2, 2026) printed AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B (+221% YoY), guided FQ4 to $21.7B (+236%), FY2026 to ~$58B, and gave line of sight to ~$115B in FY2027 and ~$230B in FY2028; it now has six XPU customers (Broadcom Form 8-K, Sep 2026; Q3 call). JPMorgan projects AI ASIC unit shipments overtaking GPUs in 2027 (12.5M vs 10.9M units).
  • End state: by 2028 a large share of frontier training and most inference runs on purpose-built silicon (40-65% claimed TCO advantage vs. GPUs at scale), sold as multi-gigawatt rack-scale systems on 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet fabrics. Merchant GPUs remain the flexible, supply-constrained other half of the market, not a casualty.
  • Investment opportunity: big-4 hyperscaler capex is guided to >$700B in 2026 (up from ~$410B) and ~$870B to >$1T in 2027 (Moody's; Evercore/BofA); Nvidia's CFO put 2027 industry capex at $1.3T. The distinction from prior hype cycles is that the buyers are cash-rich. The part that rhymes with prior cycles is the marginal buyer (frontier labs), whose demand is increasingly underwritten by vendor and capital-market financing.
  • Critical context 1: this is not a zero-sum fight with Nvidia in 2027. Nvidia's FQ2 FY2027 revenue was $96.2B (+106%) and it guided $108B; both camps are supply-constrained (CoWoS fully booked through 2026, HBM sold out), so the scarce inputs, not the chip architecture, set who earns the rents.
  • Critical context 2: the macro regime turned against long-duration assets in September: the Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on Sep 16, 2026 (first hike since 2023) and the 10-year Treasury reportedly touched ~5.2-5.3% (highest since 2007), while semiconductor ETFs absorbed a record ~$46B of inflows in 2026.

  • No rule change created the custom-silicon market; the shift is commercial, not regulatory.
  • Nvidia's Q3 FY2027 guidance explicitly excludes China data center compute, with Hopper under 1% of Q2 data center revenue (Nvidia press release, Aug 26, 2026): export controls are a closed door for the whole stack.
  • Power is the binding policy-adjacent constraint: PJM came up 6,625 MW short in its December 2025 capacity auction and summer 2027 is the first season it expects insufficient capacity (Introl; Gartner).

Behavioral Change — ✅ Firing

  • Broadcom and Marvell together hold ~95% of the custom ASIC co-design market (Broadcom ~60%, Marvell ~35%; Tom's Hardware, May 2026).

4. TAM Expansion Analysis

4a. Market Size & Growth Drivers

This theme creates net-new demand: workloads that were uneconomic on general-purpose GPUs (frontier inference at hundreds of millions of users) become viable when the buyer owns the architecture. The numbers that matter come from company guidance, and they run far ahead of third-party market-research forecasts, which look stale.

  • Broadcom AI semiconductor revenue: ~$58B (FY2026 guide) → ~$115B (FY2027) → ~$230B (FY2028) (Broadcom Q3 FY2026 call, Sep 2026). Q4 guide $21.7B (+236% YoY).
  • Marvell total revenue: ~$12B (FY2027) → ~$18B (FY2028); custom silicon expected to more than double in FY28 (Marvell, Aug 27, 2026).
  • Third-party forecast for contrast: custom AI ASIC market $9.25B (2025) → $68.4B (2032), 33% CAGR (MarketsandMarkets). Broadcom alone is guiding above this by FY2027, so treat research-house TAMs as a floor, not a ceiling.
  • AI accelerator units: 23.3M in 2027, of which 12.5M ASIC/XPU (53%) and 10.9M GPU (47%) (JPMorgan, via KuCoin News, 2026).
  • Hyperscaler capex: >$700B (2026), ~$870B (2027, Moody's) to >$1T (Evercore / BofA) and $1.3T industry-wide (Nvidia CFO) (DatacenterDynamics; Crypto Briefing, 2026).
  • Nvidia FQ2 FY2027 revenue $96.2B (+106%), data center $89B; FQ3 guide $108B (Nvidia, Aug 26, 2026).

Structural drivers: the cost per token is the binding economic variable for labs and hyperscalers, and bespoke silicon plus Ethernet fabrics lower it; power and packaging scarcity force buyers to maximize useful work per watt and per CoWoS slot. Cyclical drivers: cheap, abundant capital (vendor loans, private credit, equity rounds into labs), the competitive 'must-have-compute' dynamic among five or six buyers, and equity-market valuations that fund the capex. The structural half is durable; the cyclical half depends on a financing window that rates at 16-year highs is now testing.
