Pillar 1 — The Business

  • Two-sentence test: SpaceX launches payloads at the lowest cost in the industry and uses that cost advantage to run Starlink, a subscription broadband constellation that is the profit engine; since the February 2026 xAI acquisition it also builds AI compute (Colossus) and sells Grok/X, which is where the capital goes.
  • Segment economics: Connectivity 2025 revenue $11.4B (61% of $18B) at a 39% operating margin ($4.4B). Q2 2026: Connectivity $4.3B, Space $962M, AI $2.56B. [S-1 via Via Satellite/Value Add VC; Q2 release]
  • Customers: 12M Starlink subscribers (2x YoY, +1.7M net adds in Q2); government/NASA/DoD for launch. AI revenue concentration is unknown — the Cursor acquisition ($60B in stock, closing Q3 2026) adds developer revenue but also ~3% dilution at current market cap.
  • Moat: WIDE — cost curve in launch (management claims 99%+ cost-to-orbit reduction), regulatory spectrum and orbital filings, vertical integration. AI is not moated: it competes with hyperscalers and OpenAI on rented/owned Nvidia silicon.
  • Governance and capital allocation: Musk holds ~39% of shares and ~84% of votes via 10:1 Class B stock; a controlled company with a founder lock-up to June 12, 2027. Related-party risk is real (xAI was bought from Musk). Capital deployed: capex $4.4B (2023) → $20.7B (2025) → ~$65B guided (2026); no buybacks or dividends. [Wikipedia/CNBC; X post citing S-1]
  • Bull: Starlink compounding at 60% EBITDA margins plus Starship cadence funds an AI position no pure-play can match. Bear: a launch company is being valued as an AI hyperscaler, with Musk-controlled governance and the AI segment burning most of the cash.
