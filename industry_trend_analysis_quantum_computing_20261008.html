Industry Trend Analysis

Quantum Computing

Buy-side thematic read · October 8, 2026 · Theme Conviction 5/10 · Peak verdict: Late-cycle (2/6 exhaustion signals firing)

1. Introduction

  • What it is: computers that use qubits (superposition and entanglement) to solve a narrow set of problems (chemistry/materials simulation, optimization, cryptanalysis) that scale badly on classical machines. Competing hardware modalities: superconducting (IBM, Google, Rigetti), trapped ion (IonQ, Quantinuum), neutral atom (QuEra, Atom Computing, Infleqtion), photonic (PsiQuantum), annealing (D-Wave), silicon spin (Diraq).
  • Why now: error correction crossed from theory to hardware in 2026. Quantinuum's Helios ran 48 logical qubits, Atom Computing entangled 24 logical qubits from 112 physical, QuEra reported 100 logical qubits on 10,000+ physical, and Google's Willow showed logical error rates falling about 2.14x with each larger surface-code lattice (The Quantum Insider; paperguide.ai, 2026). The state also became a buyer: Commerce signed $2.01B of CHIPS letters of intent with nine companies on May 21, 2026, taking minority equity stakes in seven (NIST; CNBC).
  • End state: fault-tolerant machines (IBM targets Starling in 2029: 200 logical qubits, 100 million gates) that make quantum chemistry and materials design routine, and a mandatory migration of public-key cryptography (a June 22, 2026 executive order sets federal post-quantum deadlines of Dec 31, 2030 for key establishment and Dec 31, 2031 for signatures) [IBM; The Hacker News].
  • Investment opportunity: the science is real but the revenue is not yet: the six listed pure-plays we screened (IONQ, RGTI, QBTS, QUBT, QNT, INFQ) have $347M of combined trailing revenue against a $41.5B combined market cap, about 119x sales [Yahoo Finance, Oct 8, 2026]. Independent 2030 market forecasts run from $2.5B (BCG baseline) to $20.2B (MarketsandMarkets); McKinsey sees up to $97B of quantum technology revenue by 2035.
  • Critical context: this is a two-clock theme. The technology clock is early (fault tolerance 2029+), while the equity clock has already run one full leg: 2026 brought a Quantinuum IPO ($1.68B, June 4), an IQM listing (July 3) and an Infleqtion listing, then a 30% pullback in the pure-plays in a month. Pure-plays are now 50-75% below their 1-year highs (IONQ $39.45 vs $82.09; RGTI $14.14 vs $56.34; QBTS $14.60 vs $44.78; QNT $41.52 vs $83.17) [Yahoo Finance].

Technology Inflection — ✅ Firing

  • Quantinuum Helios reached 48 logical qubits on trapped-ion hardware; Microsoft and Quantinuum created 12 highly reliable logical qubits and ran an end-to-end chemistry simulation within chemical accuracy (The Quantum Insider, Jun 13, 2026).
  • Google's Willow (105 physical qubits) showed logical error rates fall about 2.14x per lattice-size step, the first hardware-scale evidence that fault-tolerance scaling curves hold (paperguide.ai, 2026).
  • QuEra's third-generation model targets 100 logical qubits on 10,000+ physical qubits (about 100:1); Atom Computing reported 24 logical from 112 physical. Ratios are not comparable across code definitions: treat as direction, not a benchmark.
  • IBM expects verified quantum advantage by end-2026 and its Kookaburra qLDPC module in 2026; Starling (200 logical qubits) is slated for 2029 (IBM).

Regulatory Shift — ✅ Firing

  • June 22, 2026 executive order: federal post-quantum key establishment by Dec 31, 2030 and digital signatures by Dec 31, 2031, pulling the 2035 target forward 4-5 years; contractors must comply by Dec 31, 2030 (The Hacker News; Sectigo).
  • May 21, 2026: Commerce announced letters of intent for $2.01B under the CHIPS Act: IBM $1B, GlobalFoundries $375M, $100M each to D-Wave, Rigetti, Infleqtion, PsiQuantum, Quantinuum and Atom Computing, Diraq up to $38M; equity stakes in seven (NIST; Manufacturing Dive).
  • Oct 7, 2026: DARPA moved Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ into Stage C of the Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (about $200M new DARPA funding plus a $150M conditional loan for PsiQuantum); IonQ's Stage C agreement is worth up to $300M (HPCwire; The Quantum Insider).
  • National Quantum Initiative Reauthorization (S.3597 / H.R.8462) cleared both committees in April 2026 but still awaits floor votes: a pending, not a completed, tailwind.

Behavioral Change — ⚠️ Partial

  • IonQ Q2 2026 revenue $80.1M (+287% YoY), RPO $485M vs $122M a year earlier; growth includes the $1.8B SkyWater acquisition, so organic growth is not separable from the filings we found (Investing.com; Globe and Mail call transcript).
  • D-Wave: Q2 revenue $3.1M (flat YoY), bookings $2.1M, H1 bookings $35.5M including a $20M Advantage2 sale to Florida Atlantic University; about 100 customers, 62.4% of revenue from commercial enterprises (D-Wave release; Quantum Computing Report).
  • Rigetti Q2 revenue $5.1M with a $28.1M operating loss (DCD, Aug 2026). IQM cites more than €102M of orders (The Quantum Insider, Aug 4).
  • Key test not yet passed: a customer paying for quantum output that beats the classical alternative on a repeatable workload. IBM's end-2026 advantage claim is the next checkpoint.

Capital Flow — ✅ Firing

  • PsiQuantum closed $1.5B at a $10.5B valuation in May 2026 and has a conditional $150M loan from the Office of Strategic Capital; Quantinuum raised $1.68B in its June 4 IPO; IQM listed July 3 (Crunchbase; CNBC).
  • Quantonation closed a €220M second fund in February 2026, the largest dedicated quantum fund (The Quantum Insider).
  • Caution: venture funding is $1.2B so far in 2026 against a record $4.1B in 2025, with the largest 2026 round a $200M financing for Photonic (Crunchbase News, 2026): late-stage and public money is replacing early-stage money.

Narrative Momentum — ✅ Firing

  • Google Quantum AI launched a neutral-atom program in Boulder under Prof. Adam Kaufman (March 2026); IonQ closed the $1.8B SkyWater deal for onshore fabrication (Quantum Insider; Investing.com).
  • NVIDIA's NVQLink connects quantum processors to GPU supercomputers, announced with 17 quantum builders and nine national labs; Quantum Machines added CUDA-Q/NVQLink support in September 2026.
  • Multiple pure-play and ETF stories a day in retail finance media (Motley Fool, 24/7 Wall St., Oct 2026): mainstream attention has arrived, which historically is late in the equity cycle.

4. TAM Expansion Analysis

4a. Market Size & Growth Drivers

Quantum computing does not displace an existing market: it opens chemistry, materials and optimization workloads that classical computers cannot economically solve, plus a forced post-quantum cryptography refresh of every network. The net-new demand is real, but forecasters disagree by an order of magnitude on how soon it is paid for, and today's revenue is a few hundred million dollars.

  • BCG baseline: about $2.5B of quantum computing revenue by 2030; optimistic scenario $5B (BCG, 2026).
  • BCC Research: $7.3B by 2030 at a 34.6% CAGR (BCC Research, Aug 2025).
  • MarketsandMarkets: about $20.2B by 2030 at a 41.8% CAGR (2025-2030).
  • McKinsey: up to $97B of quantum technology revenue by 2035, of which about $72B is quantum computing.
  • Today: six listed pure-plays have $347M combined TTM revenue against a $41.5B combined market cap (about 119x); that cap is 8.3x BCG's optimistic 2030 market and 2.1x the most bullish 2030 forecast above (Yahoo Finance, Oct 8, 2026; our computation).

Structural drivers: the 2030/2031 federal post-quantum deadlines are legally dated and will spend regardless of qubit progress; the $2.01B CHIPS package and DARPA's QBI are funded; chemistry simulation has a clear value ceiling if fault tolerance arrives (IBM 2029). Cyclical drivers: retail enthusiasm, equity-funded balance sheets (ATM offerings), and the timing of commercial advantage claims, which are management-reported and unaudited.
