Industry Trend Analysis

Physical AI

Buy-side thematic read · October 8, 2026 · Theme Conviction 5/10 · Peak verdict: Peaking

1. Introduction

  • What it is: physical AI is AI that perceives and acts in the physical world — humanoid and mobile robots, warehouse and factory automation — driven by robot foundation models trained in simulation and on real-world data, running on edge compute, and moved by actuators.
  • Why now: robot foundation models and edge compute have stepped up together: Nvidia's Isaac GR00T N2 'World Action Model' is reported at more than 2x leading models on generalization benchmarks, Jetson Thor delivers 7.5x the compute of Orin, and the Jetson T4000 module is priced at $1,999 in volume (Nvidia GTC 2026, CES 2026 coverage).
  • Real volumes, mostly Chinese: roughly 20,000 humanoids shipped in 2025 with China ~85–90% of them (Bank of America); in H1 2026 AgiBot shipped ~9,700 units (43% share) and Unitree ~5,900 (31%), with shipments up ~300% YoY (Counterpoint Research).
  • End state: general-purpose robots doing repetitive factory, warehouse and eventually service work at a price below annual labor cost. Goldman Sachs models 75,000 humanoid units in 2026, 890,000 in 2030 and 6.5M in 2035 ($138B market); Morgan Stanley sees 13M in service by 2035 (Goldman, Morgan Stanley via humanoid.guide, 2026).
  • Investment opportunity and the catch: robotics start-ups raised $18.8B in the first half of 2026 — more than all of 2025 and the 2021 peak year (Futurism/Crunchbase-based reporting), but combined Western humanoid deployments are still in the hundreds. This is a real technology cycle with a financing cycle running ahead of it.
  • Definitional note: 'physical AI' is broader than humanoids — industrial arms, warehouse automation and delivery robots are included; this note focuses on the humanoid and robot-foundation-model core and treats classic automation as an adjacent layer.

Technology Inflection — ✅ Firing

  • Foundation models: Nvidia previewed Isaac GR00T N2 at GTC 2026, a 'World Action Model' architecture reported to outperform leading vision-language-action models by more than 2x on generalization benchmarks; Physical Intelligence's π0/π0.5 models extended open-world generalization (Nvidia, Physical Intelligence, 2026).
  • Edge compute: Jetson Thor offers 7.5x the compute and 3.5x the efficiency of Jetson Orin; the Jetson T4000 delivers 1,200 FP4 TFLOPS at 70W and is priced at $1,999 per 1,000-unit volume (CES 2026 coverage).
  • Cost curve: Unitree's R1 is ~$6,000 and the G1 ~$16,000; humanoid prices are falling roughly 40% a year in secondary estimates (buyer's guides, 2026) — a non-linear break versus the $30,000–$150,000 estimated production cost of Western industrial units.
  • Caveat: actuators can be up to 60% of a humanoid's bill of materials, so compute and models are not the binding constraint (see Layer 6).

Regulatory Shift — ⚠️ Partial

  • Standards in progress: humanoid safety is covered today by ISO 10218:2025 and ANSI/A3 R15.06-2025; ISO 25785-1, for balancing humanoids in dynamic environments, is still in development, and OSHA applies existing rules to walking robots (2026 compliance guides).
  • Door closing on supply: China placed seven medium and heavy rare earths and the magnets that contain them under export licensing in April 2025; a further escalation (October 2025) is suspended only until Nov 10, 2026 (rare-earth-mining.com, Oregon Group, 2026).
  • No demand-side mandate: unlike EVs or solar, there is no subsidy or mandate creating humanoid demand in the sources found, so this scores Partial.
  • Standards as an eventual gate: a mature humanoid standard would allow humans-and-robots-together deployments at scale; its absence is why factory pilots are fenced.

Behavioral Change — ⚠️ Partial

  • Deployments exist, at small scale: Figure's robot spent 11 months on BMW's Spartanburg line contributing to more than 30,000 vehicles; Agility's Digit moves totes at GXO and Amazon and signed Toyota (Feb 2026) and Mercado Libre; Apptronik runs pilots at Mercedes-Benz, GXO and Jabil (2026 deployment roundups).
  • Large intent: Hyundai plans more than 25,000 Atlas units across Hyundai and Kia plants by 2028 and 30,000 a year from 2028, but all 2026 units are assigned internally and one report puts current output near four a month (Hyundai CES 2026; drweb.de).
  • Tesla scale: about 300 Optimus units were deployed for learning at the Q1 2026 call; V3 production was targeted for late July–August 2026 at Fremont after the Model S/X line ended in May (Tesla Q1 2026 call coverage).
  • The missing test: combined active deployments are measured in the hundreds of units — the pilot-to-operating-budget conversion that makes behavior irreversible has not been demonstrated.

Capital Flow — ✅ Firing

  • Venture: robotics start-ups pulled in $18.8B in H1 2026, more than all of 2025 and the 2021 peak year (Futurism/aiinsightsnews, mid-2026).
  • Concentration: Skild AI raised $1.4B in January 2026 (valuation above $14B); Apptronik closed $935M of Series A at a $5.5B valuation; Physical Intelligence is reported in talks for ~$1B at above $11B; Figure AI is valued at $39B with Nvidia, Brookfield, Qualcomm and Intel among investors; Neura Robotics closed up to $1.4B.
  • Public markets: Unitree priced its STAR Market IPO at 150.80 yuan on Aug 6, 2026, raising 6.8B yuan; shares rose 629% on day one (liga.net, CGTN, Aug 2026).
  • Corporate capital: Hyundai's plan to build 30,000 Atlas a year from 2028 and Tesla's Fremont conversion are balance-sheet commitments, not venture money.

Narrative Momentum — ✅ Firing

  • Product launches: KraneShares KOID (June; +28% through Sept 30), Roundhill HUMN (end of June; +20% by Sept 30) and Themes BOTT (August) all listed in a three-month window (Barchart/Nasdaq, fundssociety, 2026).

  • Bubble warnings are already mainstream: a venture capitalist warned of 'the next financial bubble' and several start-ups have quietly pivoted from general-purpose humanoids to niche use cases (Futurism, 2026).
  • Read: when the skeptics and the ETF issuers arrive in the same quarter, narrative has moved from a leading to a late indicator.

4. TAM Expansion Analysis

4a. Market Size & Growth Drivers

Physical AI unlocks labor that software could not touch: repetitive factory, warehouse and logistics tasks currently done by people, valued at the wage bill rather than at software prices. A $160,000 loaded labor cost replaced by a robot with a 5-year total cost of $32,000–$40,000 is the unit-economics claim behind the models below; none of that is yet demonstrated at fleet scale.

  • Goldman Sachs: 75,000 humanoid units shipped in 2026, 890,000 in 2030, 6.5M in 2035; 2035 market $138B — after raising its 2035 estimate roughly sixfold in value from $6B to $38B in an earlier revision (humanoid.guide, 2026).
  • Morgan Stanley: China shipments of 50,000 humanoids in 2026 (doubled), 446,000 a year by 2030 ($15B); ~13M in service by 2035 and a $5T market by 2050 (humanoid.guide, cryptobriefing, 2026).
  • Markets and Markets: humanoid robot market $5.41B in 2026 → $50.27B by 2035, 28.1% CAGR; AI-in-robotics $20.4B (2025) → $182.7B (2033), 32% CAGR (2026).
  • Actual volumes: ~20,000 humanoids shipped in 2025 (Bank of America); H1 2026 global shipments ≈ 22,500 units (derived from AgiBot's 9,700 = 43%; Counterpoint); China output expected above 100,000 in 2026 (TrendForce).
  • Gap check: Goldman's 75,000 for 2026 requires H2 shipments of ~52,500, or 2.3x H1 — a demanding second half.

Structural vs cyclical. Structural: labor shortages in manufacturing and logistics, aging workforces, and the physics that a humanoid form factor fits human-built spaces; falling component and model costs are also structural. Cyclical: today's volumes are heavily Chinese and pilot-driven, venture and IPO capital is at a cyclical high, and orders from OEMs such as Hyundai are captive plans rather than market-priced demand. The honest TAM is large but the near-term revenue pool is small — Markets and Markets sizes 2026 at $5.4B, a rounding error against venture valuations of $39B for one company.

6. Peak & Reversal Watch — When to Be Concerned

Peak Verdict: 3/6 exhaustion signals firing (valuation and crowding, demand-side funding, macro) — Peaking. Estimated time to peak: the financing/valuation cycle is at or near peak now, so the next 2–4 quarters decide whether it rolls over; the underlying technology and deployment cycle is early and would not peak before 2028 or later. This reconciles with Section 2's 'Early' fundamental stage: deployments are in the hundreds to tens of thousands, but venture marks, an open IPO window, ETF launches and a 5.3% 10-year are late-cycle conditions. The risk is not that the theme fails but that capital is repriced years ahead of revenue, as in 3D printing.

6e. Closest Historical Analogue

The closest analogue is 3D printing in 2013–2014: genuine technology and enterprise pilots, sky-high valuations (3D Systems flirted with $100 a share), and a crash when sales fell short of expectations. Stratasys fell almost 90% from September 2014 to February 2016, and both it and 3D Systems were still more than 80% below their all-time highs years later. Physical AI differs in that the technology step (foundation models) is real, volumes are already tens of thousands of units a year and Chinese makers are shipping, but it resembles 3D printing in that demand has not yet moved from innovation budgets into operating budgets. The comparison is unflattering because venture marks of $39B for one company sit above anything 3D printing's leaders ever saw.
